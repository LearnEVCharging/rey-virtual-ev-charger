Station profile — the nameplate the station reports to the backend

Connector count is reported to your backend (e.g. NumberOfConnectors); the live demo drives one connector at a time.

A built-in OCPP 2.0.1 CSMS runs on the server — just hit Connect and drive the station. No backend of your own needed.

Enter your CSMS's wss:// URL, a station id, and a Basic-auth password if it needs one. The relay presents the auth headers your browser can't.

ℹ️ Free version: if the station sits idle for a while it may be put to sleep, so a session can drop after a period of inactivity — it wakes again within a few seconds on reconnect. While Rey is free, there's no cap on how much you can use it.

Plug & Charge is an OCPP 2.0.1+ feature — switch the version above to try it.

The CSMS can also drive the station — send it a remote start/stop, a Reset, or a TriggerMessage and watch Rey respond in the log.

Local auth list

Whitelist an RFID/eMAID and the charge point authorizes it locally — no Authorize is sent to the backend (that's how a station keeps working offline). Unknown tokens still go to the CSMS.

Device model

Connect to load the device model. Edit a value to change it on the station, or send GetVariables/SetVariables from the CSMS.

Certificates

The CSMS drives provisioning: the charge point generates a real RSA key + CSR (SignCertificate), the CSMS installs the root trust anchor (InstallCertificate) and returns the signed leaf chain (CertificateSigned). Watch every frame in the log. On OCPP 1.6 this is the Security Whitepaper extension (ExtendedTriggerMessage).
